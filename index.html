CHECKEDIN

A Web and Mobile Application for Attendance Monitoring and Event Management in the College of Computer Studies

A digital platform designed to support secure attendance verification, event coordination, real-time monitoring, and participation tracking in academic and organizational activities.

01 / Research overview

Project background

CHECKEDIN addresses challenges associated with recording, verifying, monitoring, and reporting attendance in College of Computer Studies classes and events.

Problem statement

Existing attendance and event processes can make it difficult to verify student presence, maintain accurate records, monitor participation, and prepare attendance reports. Manual recording and limited monitoring can add administrative effort for faculty members and organization officers.

General objective

To design, develop, and evaluate CHECKEDIN during Academic Year 2026–2027 to improve the accuracy, security, efficiency, and reliability of attendance monitoring through secure attendance verification, automated reporting, real-time monitoring, and game-style participation incentives.

02 / Research objectives

Specific objectives

The project is guided by three objectives covering system development, functional testing, and evaluation.

1

Design and develop the system

Develop a web and mobile attendance monitoring and event management system with:

  • Secure attendance verification using expiring QR codes and time-limited OTPs
  • Event creation and management
  • Attendance reports for professors based on assigned subjects and sections, and for organizational events
  • Role-based access for administrators, professors, and organization officers
  • Game-style incentives to encourage student participation
2

Test system functionality

Test major features through Black Box Testing, including registration and authentication, role access, QR and OTP verification, event management, real-time attendance monitoring, attendance records, reports, notifications, participation points, and account management.

3

Evaluate quality and user acceptance

Assess the system through questionnaire-based evaluation using software quality criteria and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM): perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, attitude toward using, and behavioral intention to use.

03 / Methodology

Agile development approach

The project follows an Agile Software Development Methodology, organizing the work into iterative stages of planning, design, development, testing, and assessment.

Stage 01Planning

Identify system needs, users, and project requirements.

Stage 02Design

Prepare system structure, interfaces, and workflows.

Stage 03Development

Implement the web and mobile application modules.

Stage 04Testing

Check expected behavior through Black Box Testing.

Stage 05Assessment

Gather questionnaire responses and evaluate quality and acceptance.

Functional testing

The manuscript reports that all 11 test cases and 70 testing steps passed in the specified Black Box Testing activities.

Evaluation dimensions

Software quality criteria include usability, functionality, reliability, efficiency, security, and user satisfaction. TAM dimensions include perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, attitude toward using, and behavioral intention to use.

Reported testing results reflect the test cases and steps documented in the research manuscript; they are not a guarantee of defect-free operation in every environment.
